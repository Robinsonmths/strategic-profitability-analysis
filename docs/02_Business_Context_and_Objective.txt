# 📊 Category Profitability Analysis Case

**Global Sales Dataset – Strategic Analysis**

---

## 1️⃣ Business Context

The company operates in the sale of physical products across multiple global markets, covering several categories, subcategories, regions, and shipping modes.
Despite a significant sales volume, there are strong indications that **real profitability is not fully transparent**, especially when discounts and shipping costs are taken into account.

Senior management aims to understand which categories truly sustain the operation and which may be **masking losses or operational inefficiencies**.

---

## 2️⃣ Analysis Objective

Evaluate the **real profitability of product categories**, considering:

* Revenue
* Sales volume
* Applied discounts
* Shipping cost
* Net profit after shipping cost

With the goal of classifying categories and products as:

* **Viable**
* **Adjustable**
* **Critical** (requiring strategic review)

---

## 3️⃣ Business Questions

* Which categories generate the highest real profit?
* Are there categories with high volume but low margins?
* Does shipping cost significantly compromise profitability in any category?
* Is the average discount, when weighted by volume, aligned with financial return?
* Are there products or categories that appear profitable but become unviable after shipping cost?

---

## 4️⃣ Analyzed Hypotheses

### H1 — Category Profitability

Some categories may show positive profit but extremely low real margins, compromising long-term sustainability.

---

### H2 — Discount vs. Volume

Discounts only make sense when associated with high sales volume.
Discounts applied to low-volume sales may erode margins without delivering operational gains.

---

### H3 — Impact of Shipping Cost on Real Profit

Shipping cost may fully offset operating profit, especially for low-ticket products or inefficient shipping models.

---

## 5️⃣ Data Assumptions and Treatment

### 🔹 Outliers

* Extreme percentages and values are not used as direct decision metrics
* Analysis focuses on totals, weighted averages, and aggregated margins

---

### 🔹 Negative Profits

* If **< 1%** of rows present negative profit → excluded, with documented caveat
* If **≈ 10% or more** → analyzed separately to identify root causes
* Initially treated as potential data issues or operational distortions, never discarded without investigation

---

## 6️⃣ Official Project Metrics

The metrics used for decision-making are:

* **Sales** — Total Revenue
* **Quantity** — Units Sold
* **Shipping Cost** — Total Shipping Cost
* **Real Profit** = Profit − Shipping Cost
* **Real Margin (%)** = Real Profit / Sales
* **Weighted Average Discount**, weighted by Quantity

⚠️ Simple average discounts are not used, as they do not reflect scale efficiency.

---

## 7️⃣ Initial Unit of Analysis

The analysis starts at the **Category** level, focusing on:

* Total volume
* Total revenue
* Total shipping cost
* Aggregated real profit
* Real margin

From this point, critical categories may be further broken down into **Sub-Category or Product-level analysis**.

---

## 8️⃣ Strategic Decision Criteria

Situation                       			 		 Action                                          

Positive profit + healthy margin  			Maintain                                        
Positive profit + low margin     			Adjust pricing, discount strategy, or logistics 
Low volume + high margin         			Caution: excessive dependency                   
Profit compromised by shipping   			Review logistics model                          
Recurring negative profit        				Review or discontinue                           

---

## 9️⃣ Expected Outcome

By the end of the project, the company will have:

* Clear visibility into which categories truly sustain the business
* Identification of hidden operational inefficiencies
* A solid analytical foundation for pricing, discount, and logistics decisions
* A **replicable analytical framework** for future analyses
