## **STEP 3 — Definition of Metrics and Analytical Logic**

### **Objective of the Step**

Define which metrics will be used, at which level of aggregation, and how each metric directly answers the business questions and hypotheses raised — **before** any calculation or visualization.

This step **does not involve code, charts, or data exploration**, only the formalization of the analytical logic.

---

### **Primary Unit of Analysis**

The analysis will start at the **Category level**.

Other levels (Subcategory, Product, Market/Region) will be used **only for drill-down**, when a category shows warning signals or opportunities.

This decision avoids fragmented analyses and ensures focus on **strategic decision-making**.

---

### **Official Project Metrics (Category Level)**

The following metrics are considered **official** and will be used as the basis for all decisions:

#### **Volume**

* **Quantity (Total)**
* Share of total volume (%)

#### **Revenue**

* **Sales (Total)**
* Share of total revenue (%)

#### **Logistics**

* **Shipping Cost (Total)**
* **Shipping Cost / Sales (%)**

#### **Profit**

* **Gross Profit**
* **Real Profit = Profit − Shipping Cost**
* **Real Margin (%) = Real Profit / Sales**

> From this point forward, the **only valid profit for decision-making is profit after shipping cost (Real Profit)**.

---

### **Discount Metric**

#### **Adopted Approach**

Discount will be analyzed as a **Quantity-Weighted Average Discount**.

#### **Justification**

* Discounts are only considered healthy when associated with **scale gains**.
* Simple average discounts distort the analysis by giving the same weight to small and large orders.

---

### **Metrics by Hypothesis**

#### **H1 — “Categories with higher average discount show significantly lower margins.”**

**Metrics used:**

* Weighted average discount
* Real margin (%)
* Comparative analysis across categories

---

#### **H2 — “A small number of categories concentrate most of the total losses.”**

**Metrics used:**

* Total Real Profit by category
* Percentage share of total losses
* Negative impact ranking

---

#### **H3 — “In some categories, shipping cost is the main driver of profit loss.”**

**Metrics used:**

* Gross Profit
* Real Profit
* Absolute and percentage difference between them
* Shipping Cost / Sales (%)

This analysis allows separating **product issues** from **operational/logistics issues**.

---

### **Supporting (Contextual) Metrics**

The following metrics **do not drive decisions in isolation**, but support interpretation:

* Average ticket (Sales / Quantity)
* Real profit per unit
* Real profit per order

---

### **Metrics and Analyses Explicitly Excluded at This Stage**

To avoid analytical noise, the following are excluded from this step:

* Time-series analysis
* Outliers as a primary metric
* Percentages without volume base
* Predictive models
* Advanced segmentations

These analyses will only be considered **if they emerge as necessary after the initial conclusions**.

---

### **Expected Outcome of Step 3**

By the end of this step, the project has:

* Clearly defined metrics
* Hypotheses directly linked to objective numbers
* Established decision unit
* Solid analytical foundation for the **Quality Gate (Step 4)**
